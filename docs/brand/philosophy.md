# Integer Fracture

A design philosophy for marks that break cleanly, and only along the grid.

**Space and form.** All form begins as a whole square. Nothing in it is drawn freehand. Every edge lands on the grid line beneath it, so a break is not a jagged accident but a staircase walked one module at a time. A fracture here is a precise act: it divides the square into exactly two pieces that still fit back together, separated by a single module of empty space. That gap is the subject of the work. The negative space between the pieces should feel as solid and deliberate as the pieces themselves, as if someone had moved the shard with tweezers and measured the distance twice.

**Color and material.** The field is very nearly black and almost silent, the colour of a screen just before it wakes. The large body is a cool, slightly warm-grey white, quiet as paper. One colour is allowed to speak: a single acid chartreuse, used on exactly one thing in any rendering of the form, the piece that has broken away. That rule cannot be broken. If chartreuse appears twice within one form, it has failed. Hairlines and annotations sit a few steps above the field, visible only to someone looking closely. The palette should look calibrated rather than chosen, every value tuned through painstaking adjustment until the contrast steps feel even.

**Scale and rhythm.** The square is built on a 16-module lattice, and that lattice is the entire rhythm. The same geometry has to hold at sixteen pixels and at sixteen hundred, so every proportion is an integer ratio of the module. Each reduction is a proof: at the smallest size each module becomes exactly one physical pixel, and the form survives with no blur, no half-pixels, and nothing approximated. Repeating the mark at decreasing sizes is the main visual device, a quiet procession that demonstrates the claim instead of stating it.

**Composition and balance.** Compositions are laid out like plates from a technical atlas: a large central specimen on its construction lattice, framed by thin corner brackets the way a capture region is framed, with small, regularly spaced studies around it. Balance comes from mass and emptiness, never from ornament. Generous margins surround everything. Elements never touch, never crowd, and never come close to the edge of the canvas. The composition should feel like it took countless hours of master-level arrangement, with each spacing value tested against its neighbours until no single one draws attention.

**Visual hierarchy and text.** The specimen comes first, its reductions second, and words a distant third. Type is thin and monospaced, set small and tracked wide, like the coordinate labels on an instrument. It names, measures and indexes, and never explains. A single wordmark may appear at a humane size, set in a precise grotesque with tight, carefully kerned spacing. Everything else whispers. The finished artifact should read as the product of deep expertise and meticulous craftsmanship, a document from a discipline that measures the space between things and treats a single module of emptiness with complete seriousness.
